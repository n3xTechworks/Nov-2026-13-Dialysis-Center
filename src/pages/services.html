Treatments

Four ways to dialyse

Each treatment does the work your kidneys can no longer do: removing waste and extra fluid. They differ in where, when and how often.

Choosing together

How we help you decide

There is rarely one right answer. We look at your health, your home and your week, then you choose.

Step 1

Learn the options

A free four-part class explains every treatment, with patients who use each one sharing their experience.

Step 2

Check what suits you

Your nephrologist reviews your heart, remaining kidney function, access and any home or travel needs.

Step 3

Start and review

We review how you feel after three months. Switching treatment later is common and always supported.

Questions

Questions about treatment

The questions new patients ask us most often. Anything else, just ask at your visit.

Which dialysis treatment is best?

Each works well when it suits the person. In-centre care suits people who want staff to do everything; home and peritoneal dialysis give more freedom; nocturnal gives gentler, longer sessions. Your nephrologist will help you weigh them up.

Will I need dialysis for the rest of my life?

Dialysis replaces kidney function when your kidneys have failed, so most people continue unless they receive a kidney transplant. If your kidneys were damaged suddenly, they sometimes recover; we check this regularly.

Can I keep working on dialysis?

Yes, many of our patients do. Evening shifts, nocturnal sessions, home haemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis are all designed to fit around a working day.

Can I get a kidney transplant from Tidewell?

We do not perform transplants, but we prepare you for assessment, refer you to transplant centres in Chennai and Bengaluru and keep dialysing you while you wait.

How soon can I start?

For planned in-centre dialysis, usually within a week of your first visit, once access and insurance approval are in place. In an emergency, your nephrologist will advise hospital admission first.
