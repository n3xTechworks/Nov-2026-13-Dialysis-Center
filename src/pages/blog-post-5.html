Travelling with dialysis: how to plan a trip

A patient's step-by-step guide to booking holiday dialysis, packing well and enjoying the journey.

Priya NairPatient writer, on haemodialysis since 2019
30 Sep 2026 · 6 min read

When I started haemodialysis in 2019 I assumed my travelling days were over. Since then I have dialysed in Kochi, Pune, Delhi, Goa and Singapore. It takes planning, but it is absolutely possible, and the Tidewell coordinators have made each trip easier. Here is the checklist I wish I had at the start.

Six to eight weeks before

Holiday dialysis slots fill up quickly, especially around festivals and school holidays. Tell your care team where and when you plan to go, then choose a centre near where you will stay. Our patient care coordinator can contact centres for you, check their availability and send your records. Ask the visiting centre about cost, payment and whether your insurance or government scheme covers sessions away from home.

Documents the visiting centre will ask for

  • A letter from your nephrologist with your diagnosis and dialysis prescription.
  • Recent blood results, usually within the last month.
  • Hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV test results, often within 30 to 90 days.
  • Your last three session reports, dry weight and access details.
  • A current medicine list and any allergies.

If you do peritoneal dialysis

PD can be easier to travel with. Many suppliers will deliver fluid to your hotel or a relative's home if you arrange it two to four weeks ahead. Pack a few days of extra supplies in case of delays, and carry a portable cycler bag or spare exchange kit in your hand luggage.

My packing list

  • All medicines for the trip plus three extra days, in their original boxes.
  • Phosphate binders in a small pouch so they are always with you at meals.
  • A copy of every document above, on paper and on your phone.
  • A light shawl for the dialysis chair, headphones and a book.
  • A refillable small bottle to help you keep track of fluids.

While you are away

Holiday food is often saltier, and hot weather makes you thirstier, so I plan one treat meal a day and keep the rest simple. I weigh myself each morning if the hotel has scales, and I keep the visiting centre's number saved alongside my home unit's.

The first trip is the hardest to plan. After that, it becomes a routine like any other.

Coming home

Bring the session reports from the visiting centre back to your home unit. Your team may repeat some blood tests, which is normal after dialysing elsewhere.

This article shares one patient's experience. Your care team will advise on what is right for you.

Key points

  • Start planning six to eight weeks before you travel.
  • Visiting centres need recent blood tests and session reports.
  • Keep medicines and documents in your hand luggage.
