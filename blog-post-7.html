Kidney health

Managing your medicines between sessions

What each common kidney medicine does, when to take it and how to keep track without the stress.

Deepa RaghavanRenal Pharmacist
16 Sep 2026 · 6 min read

Most people on dialysis take between six and twelve different medicines. Each one has a job, and many have to be timed around meals or dialysis sessions. Getting them right protects your heart, bones and blood, and helps you feel better day to day. Here is a plain-language guide to the most common ones.

Common medicines and what they do

Phosphate binders

Calcium acetate, sevelamer and lanthanum bind phosphorus in food so your body cannot absorb it. They only work when taken with the first few bites of a meal or snack that contains protein. Taking them on an empty stomach does nothing.

Active vitamin D and calcium

Calcitriol or alfacalcidol help control parathyroid hormone and protect your bones. Your dose is adjusted using your monthly calcium, phosphate and PTH results.

Anaemia treatment

Erythropoietin (EPO) injections and iron, often given during dialysis, help your body make red blood cells. Tell us if you feel more tired or breathless than usual.

Blood pressure medicines

Some people are asked to skip or delay a dose before dialysis to avoid low blood pressure during the session. Never change this yourself; ask your nephrologist what is right for you.

Medicines to check before taking

  • Painkillers such as ibuprofen, diclofenac and naproxen can damage remaining kidney function and raise potassium. Paracetamol is usually safer.
  • Some antibiotics and diabetes medicines need lower doses on dialysis.
  • Herbal and ayurvedic remedies, tonics and supplements may contain potassium, phosphate or heavy metals.
  • Ordinary multivitamins can contain vitamin A and minerals you do not need; use a renal vitamin if prescribed.

Staying organised

  1. Keep one up-to-date medicine list on paper and in the patient portal.
  2. Use a weekly pill organiser with morning, noon, evening and night sections.
  3. Keep a few binders in your bag, wallet or car for meals out.
  4. Set phone alarms for doses you often forget.
  5. Bring all your medicines to your six-monthly review so we can tidy up the list.

If you are unsure about any medicine, ask before you take it, not after.

If you miss a dose

For most medicines, take the missed dose when you remember unless it is nearly time for the next one; never double up. Missed binders cannot be made up later. If you miss blood pressure, heart or diabetes medicines regularly, tell us so we can simplify your plan.

This article is general information. Always follow the prescription from your own care team.

Key points

  • Phosphate binders work only when taken with food.
  • Some blood pressure tablets may be skipped before a session; ask first.
  • Check every new medicine, herbal remedy or painkiller with us.
